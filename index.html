About & Philosophy

“I am a student of life. Experience has not made me less curious; it has taught me to ask better questions, test my assumptions, and turn what I learn into results. The more responsibility I earn, the more deliberately I keep learning.”

I began by learning how individual parts work. Then I learned how parts become products, how manufacturing systems create value, and how people, budgets, decisions and technology determine whether a project succeeds. Today I work at the intersection of engineering, operations and intelligent systems.

My method is consistent at every stage: establish the baseline, identify the governing constraint, compare technical alternatives, build a controllable delivery plan, validate performance with evidence, and transfer a sustainable process to the people operating it.

Looking ahead, I’m focused on the next generation of AI in manufacturing — and the real challenges that come with it. The hard part is no longer the model; it’s grounding it in governed data and shared KPI semantics, keeping a human in the loop, proving reliability on the factory floor, and scaling from a working prototype to trusted, auditable decision support. I believe the growth comes less from bigger models and more from disciplined integration — connecting engineering, operations and intelligent systems so they work as one.

Mechanical Design2019 – 2020 · NHCE

Eco-Friendly Heated Exhaust Muffler

A B.E. team project exploring whether a modified muffler could support secondary oxidation of residual exhaust constituents — unburned hydrocarbons and CO — while preserving acceptable flow. Integrated an electrically heated zone, perforated flow path, internal baffles and a supplemental-air inlet in a ~750 mm shell, tested on a 110 cc four-stroke scooter.

  • Thermofluid design — gas velocity, Reynolds number and friction-loss estimates, balancing residence time vs. engine backpressure.
  • Reported result — up to 32.7% lower carbon-particle opacity in the future-work excerpt (not certified emissions compliance).
  • Lesson — connecting thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, CAD, manufacturability and test planning instead of optimizing one parameter in isolation.

Manufacturing SystemsUMBC · ENME 617

Seabin — Marine Debris Filtration System

An Advanced Manufacturing Process project: a floating-debris collection concept for marinas, dockyards and calm water. A submerged pump induces surface flow that draws floating trash into a removable catch bag and discharges screened water back to the body of water — designed from concept through a full manufacturing and deployment plan.

  • Materials & process — HDPE body (injection molding), neoprene seal (compression molding), fiber-reinforced piping (filament winding); PRIMA selection for a 1,000–10,000 units/yr band.
  • Risk & quality — design FMEA prioritizing catch bag (RPN 180), suction pump (RPN 250) and power supply (RPN 480) with protective controls.
  • Industrialization — Kanban production layout and a Shanghai-to-U.S.-port logistics scenario.

AI Superintendent — Plant Signals into Decisions

A manufacturing decision-support prototype answering one question: can we make a plant leader faster and more accurate at diagnosing a shift without switching between five systems or reconciling definitions by hand? Natural-language questions are grounded in a governed KPI glossary and source-linked data.

  • Architecture — extract → normalize asset/line/shift IDs → define KPI semantics → model relationships between orders, downtime, labor and output.
  • Example — “Why did Line 2 miss target last night?” → reconcile downtime, compare labor to MOST standard, surface loss categories with source links.
  • Reported — ~20% better downtime-reporting visibility (prototype, with human-in-the-loop review — not an autonomous agent).
